# Logic audit — 2026-09-22

Does the desk's logic hold together, end to end? Six lanes: decisions and scoring, capture and
ops, the GEX engine, research, terminal, and cross-module contracts.

**Method.** Every finding here was confirmed in **both** the code and the data. The rule comes
from [state-review-2026-09-21-verification.md](state-review-2026-09-21-verification.md): a
data-only reading is a symptom, and that review's inferred causes were wrong twice. The data is
the live homeserver database, read over the read-only MCP connector on the evening of
2026-09-22 (session 2026-09-22 captured). Each finding carries a `file:line`, a reproducing query
and the task it was filed as. **Nothing was fixed**: the project rule is to file, not fix
silently.

**Headline.** The GEX engine, its stored rollups and the research gates hold up. **The decision
track record does not.** Its best-looking key, `FADE_CALL_WALL` at +0.89R, is mostly produced by
an evaluation bug and by counting one trade twice. Under a conservative re-score it is roughly
flat to negative. That number is what the market-research skill tells a human to lean on.

## A1 — Fade outcomes exit at a price printed before the fill · critical · T115

`scan/outcomes.py` replays daily bars against a fade's resting limit. When a bar touches the
entry, the hold loop starts **on that same bar** (`for j in range(trigger_index, n)`, `:236`).
It checks the target against the bar's full high/low (`:244`) and, on a hit, exits at
`min(target, open)` for a short or `max(target, open)` for a long (`:267`).

The **open** of the trigger bar printed *before* price travelled to the limit. For a limit that
did not gap, that open cannot be an exit price. The docstring promises the opposite ("the track
record understates rather than flatters the engine", `:14`). Stop-before-target (`:246`) handles
the stop/target ambiguity, but not the fill/target ambiguity.

The data: **every one of the five scored fade wins** filled at the wall (not gapped) and
"resolved" on the trigger bar.

| id | trade | trigger bar (O / H / L / C) | what the scorer did | scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | XOP short 200, target 195 | 09-15: 194.7 / 200.9 / 194.5 / 199.7 | exited at the 194.7 open, then filled at 200 | +2.51R |
| 41 | same order, re-emitted | same bar | same | +2.47R |
| 28 | GLD long 390, target 395 | 09-16: 398.9 / 400.6 / 388.4 / 391.7 | exited at the 398.9 open, then filled at 390 | +2.26R |
| 24 | USO short 160, target 155 | 09-14: 159.4 / 160.3 / 154.8 / 156.7 | target on the fill bar; order unknowable | +2.26R |
| 47 | same order, re-emitted | same bar | same | +2.13R |

`mfe_r` / `mae_r` share the defect: the trigger bar's excursion is measured from before the fill
(`:238-241`). T112's calibration was going to fit stops and targets against exactly these
columns.

**Re-scored conservatively** — on a non-gapped trigger bar only the stop can resolve, and
scoring resumes on the next bar — using the stored bars:

- 19 / 41 still win, a day later, at +2.38R.
- 28 wins at +2.64R: the next bar gapped above the target.
- 24 / 47 become **stops at −1.0R**: USO's next bar printed 163.4 through the stop.

`FADE_CALL_WALL` moves from **+0.89R to about +0.07R** (n = 8). Counted once per real trade
(A2), it is **about −0.14R** (n = 6). `FADE_PUT_WALL` moves slightly up. The re-score is one
defensible rule, not the fix; the fix task decides the rule.

Repro:
```sql
SELECT id, key, underlying, entry, fill, target, triggered_on, resolved_on, result_r
FROM gex.decisions
WHERE key LIKE 'FADE%' AND outcome = 'target' AND resolved_on = triggered_on;
```
There is no test for either case (grep `tests/` for a trigger-bar target: none).

## A2 — One order, scored as several trades · high · T116

A fade's resting limit is re-emitted every session while the wall stands. Each emission is its
own decision row, and each is scored. Two of the five wins above are the same XOP order
(ids 19 and 41, emitted 09-10 and 09-11, both filled on 09-15). Two more are the same USO order
(24 and 47). Across all keys, **eleven pairs** of scored decisions on the same symbol and key
overlap in time:

- `CONTINUATION_DOWN`: 7
- `FADE_CALL_WALL`: 2
- `CONTINUATION_UP`: 1
- `FADE_PUT_WALL`: 1

`gex_track_record` reports `se` as though every row were independent, so the error bar is
narrower than the evidence. T93's factor cap marks cross-symbol duplicates at emission, but
nothing reaches the record.

Repro:
```sql
SELECT a.key, count(*) FROM gex.decisions a JOIN gex.decisions b
  ON a.underlying = b.underlying AND a.key = b.key AND a.id < b.id
 AND a.result_r IS NOT NULL AND b.result_r IS NOT NULL AND b.triggered_on <= a.resolved_on
GROUP BY 1;
```

## A3 — Mislabelled history still counts · medium · T117

T99 deliberately left `gex.decisions` append-only, so its six wrong-sign rows stay in history.
That was correct. Nothing, though, keeps them *out of the record*:

- **23** (SLV, labelled `FADE_CALL_WALL`, on a −7.5mn strike) and **32** (XLE, labelled
  `FADE_PUT_WALL`, on a +51.5mn strike) are scored: −0.23R and −1R, under keys whose thesis they
  contradict.
- **50, 77, 78 and 80** are pending and will be scored the same way.

The record has no append-only way to say "this row is history, not evidence".

Repro: the wrong-sign join in
[plans/desk-integrity/00-wall-identity.md](../plans/desk-integrity/00-wall-identity.md), run
against `gex_by_strike` at `entry`.

## B1 — Session coverage keyed on the vendor timestamp's date · medium · T118

`captured_at` is Cboe's own `timestamp` field (`providers/cboe.py:293`), not the time we fetched.
For thin names it lags by hours; `outage.py` documents XBI at 4h21m.

On 09-22 the 16:45 extended capture fetched an XBI chain stamped **07:18 ET that morning**.
`session_date` correctly assigned it to 09-21, so **XBI has no 09-22 session at all**, and no
extended job retries later (T109).

The core safety net would miss the same case. `catchup.has_eod_snapshot_today`
(`jobs/catchup.py:79-98`) asks for an `is_eod` row whose `captured_at` falls on today's NY
date. A pre-open-stamped chain satisfies that while belonging to yesterday, so the 20:00 retry
would treat the session as covered. T102 added `session_date` for exactly this question, and
T104's watcher uses it. The guard that decides whether to retry does not.

Repro:
```sql
SELECT underlying, captured_at, session_date, is_eod
FROM gex.snapshots WHERE underlying = 'XBI' AND session_date >= '2026-09-21';
```

## F1 — `desk_status` misstates what `captured_at` is · medium · T119

The freshness tool the skill tells every session to call first says:

> "`session_date` is the session the contents belong to; `captured_at` is the wall clock."

`mcp/server.py:368`. It is the vendor's timestamp (B1). This is the tool whose job is the
caveats, and here the caveat is the error. It also means the desk never records **when it
fetched** a chain, which is the fact an operator wants when a symbol goes quiet.

## E1 — The brief's point-in-time promise leaks · low · T120

The brief's header promises:

> "Every figure below was read with an explicit `as_of` … nothing in it had been published later."

Three reads are unfiltered:

- `brief.py:322`, which counts the policy-path trade dates;
- `:530`, the observation total;
- `:558`, the provenance table.

For a past `as_of`, the first of these decides whether the "Implied policy path" section reports
itself unavailable, so a later vintage can change a past brief's text. The core reader
(`store/query.py:_GET_SQL`) is correct — latest vintage `≤ as_of`, one per `value_date` — and so is
`api/edges.py`'s policy query.

## D1 — `Registry` does not refuse SQLite · low · T121

Invariant 9 says `Registry` "raises rather than falling back to a local SQLite file". There is no
fallback, but there is no refusal either. `Registry.__init__` (`research/registry.py:96-112`)
accepts whatever `DATABASE_URL` resolves to, and `_insert_ignore` keeps a working SQLite branch
(`:86-89`) for the tests. `DATABASE_URL=sqlite:///x.db` would start a second registry with
nothing going red — the fork the invariant exists to prevent. A dialect check when no
`session_factory` is injected closes it.

## Checked and sound

Stated so the next audit can skip these, or re-run the query and see what changed.

- **Stop/target geometry.** 0 of 125 decisions have a stop or target on the wrong side of entry.
  Only `active` rows are scored; `watch` rows never are. There is never more than one decision
  per session × symbol × key (no per-filter duplication).
- **Wall identity since T99.** No decision emitted on 09-22 sits on a wrong-sign strike.
  `GAMMA_PIN` has not fired because its precondition (spot above the call wall, within
  `PIN_REACH_ATR`) held for no symbol on 09-22. That is not a defect.
- **Rollup consistency.** For every 09-22 EOD snapshot, `gex_levels.net_gex` equals the sum of
  `gex_by_strike.net_gex`, the sum of its four horizon columns, and the sum of `gex_by_expiry`,
  to the million.
- **IV columns (T103).** Every 09-21/22 snapshot with OI carries an ATM IV in a plausible range
  (HYG 0.037 to 0.49). The two first-of-session captures without OI are null, not zero.
- **Dealer sign (invariant 2).** One `sign` column, consumed once per value in `contract_gex`,
  `gamma_profile` and the diagnostics. The invariant's wording was corrected in `CLAUDE.md`; the
  code was right.
- **Research gates.** `paper.promote` enforces the noise-ceiling fraction, the doubled-cost
  Sharpe, the neighbourhood median, the walk-forward gate and a correlation cap on every
  promotion. Promotion at half the ceiling (`research.yaml`
  `min_fraction_of_ceiling: 0.5`) is deliberate: the watchlist *is* the forward test.
  `run_backtest` lags positions one bar; the only `shift(-1)` (`day_of_week`) reads the
  calendar, not prices.
- **Point-in-time reads.** `store/query.py` and the policy-path query select the latest vintage
  at or before `as_of`.
